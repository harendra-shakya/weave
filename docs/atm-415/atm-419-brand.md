## Positioning: Collecting Without the Chain

**Statement:** The joy of owning a numbered edition — without wallets, gas, or speculation.

**Why it's true:** No wallet. No seed phrase. No crypto. Just an edition, its provenance, and your shelf. Ownership framed as a collecting experience, not a trade.

**What it rejects:**
- Web3 speculation culture ("floor price," flip-for-profit)
- Wallet / seed-phrase onboarding
- Gas fees and transaction friction
- Anonymous token IDs and opaque metadata

**Trust mechanism (product):** *Provenance.* Every edition shows a clear, human record — edition number, artist, and origin — so the collector always knows exactly what they hold.

**Emotional payoff (product):** *The shelf.* The owned-items view is the heart of the app — visible, arrangeable, personal. Collecting you can see, not collecting hidden in a wallet.

## Name: Vitrine

- **Canonical form:** `Vitrine`
- **Pronunciation:** "vi-TREEN" — a glass-fronted display case for prized objects
- **Meaning:** The case collectors put their treasures in: visible, protected, arranged with pride. Makes the "shelf you can actually see" position literal.
- **Why it's crypto-safe:** It's the opposite of "wallet/vault" — a display case, not a lockbox. Museum/collector world, zero web3 scent.
- **Stretch:** Works for any collectible category without renaming.
- **Wordmark potential:** A glass-case frame motif; the "V" as a display stand; museum-label aesthetics.

## Narrative: The Frustration Story

**Premise:** You love owning a numbered edition from an artist you follow — but every "digital ownership" product drags you through wallets, seed phrases, gas fees, and a speculation culture you never asked for. The category made collecting feel like trading. Vitrine is collecting given back to collectors.

**Hero (homepage):**
> # Own the edition. Skip the wallet.
> Numbered digital editions with clear provenance and a shelf you're proud of — no wallet, no gas, no speculation.

**About-page opening:**
> We wanted the simple joy of owning #7 of 50 from an artist we love. Instead we got seed phrases, gas fees, and a feed full of people flipping art like stock. So we built the opposite: numbered editions, clear provenance, a shelf you're proud of — and not a single wallet in sight.

**The hero (who the audience identifies with):** The collector who wants the "#7 of 50" feeling and the pride of a visible collection — and is tired of crypto friction and speculation culture.

**Proof points (must stay true in the product):**
- Every edition shows its **number** (#X of Y) and **provenance/artist**.
- **No wallet, no seed phrase, no gas** anywhere in the flow.
- The **shelf/owner view** is real and central, not an afterthought.

## Mood: Museum Label

The quiet authority of a gallery. Generous white space, precise typography, each piece treated like a catalogued work with a proper label (edition, artist, provenance) — the antithesis of neon web3 UI.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Base** | Gallery off-white / soft grey |
| **Text** | Near-black |
| **Accent** | **Deep teal** — single restrained accent for labels/CTAs/edition marks *(swap to oxblood if too cool)* |
| **Saturation** | Calm, muted, never neon |
| **Type — titles** | Refined serif (museum-catalog feel) |
| **Type — labels/UI** | Precise grotesque sans |
| **Imagery** | Each collectible framed and centered like a hung work; museum-style caption blocks; edition number set like a catalogue raisonné entry |
| **Reference feel** | MoMA / gallery sites, Artsy (pared back), Cooper Hewitt, fine-art auction catalogs |

**What this rejects:** Neon web3 gradients, dark "vault" UI, glow effects, purple/cyan crypto palettes, hype badges, "$" price-ticker framing.

## Voice quick-reference

- **Calm, cultured, collector-first.** Gallery, not trading desk.
- **Provenance over price.** Talk about the artist, the edition, the story — never value or ROI.
- **Anti-crypto by omission, not by lecture.** Say "no wallet, no gas" once (it's the promise), then just *be* calm — don't rant about web3.
- **Honest about synthetic.** The nonclaim is stated plainly where required (footer + first purchase), not hidden or spun.
- **Never use:** "mint," "token," "chain," "wallet-as-feature," "floor," "$", "gas," "drop-and-flip," moon/ape/degen slang.

## Synthetic-only nonclaim (required by the issue)

**Placement (locked):** Footer + first-purchase notice.
- **Footer** — persistent one-line disclaimer on every page (see copy above).
- **First checkout** — a one-time notice the user sees before their first "Add to collection," stating it's synthetic with no real ownership/value.
- Main storefront UI stays immersive (the collecting fantasy is intact), but the honesty is provable and always one glance away.
- Every learning-loop verdict (GO/ITERATE/PIVOT/STOP) must also carry the explicit synthetic-only nonclaim in its written output.
