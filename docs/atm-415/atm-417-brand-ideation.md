# ATM-417 Brand Ideation — Local Video Storefront

**Brand / project:** ATM-417 WEAVE V5 App 1 (indie short-film storefront)
**Date:** 2026-07-22
**Inputs used:** ATM-417 issue (persona + problem), ATM-415 parent scope
**Constraint note:** This is a WEAVE synthetic/public-safe sandbox app. Domain/trademark/social-handle checks below are *provisional and illustrative only* — no real registration, distribution, or commerce happens. Names are chosen for fit and testability, not to claim availability.

---

## Recap: what we are ideating against

- **Audience:** An indie filmmaker (and, adjacently, the film-obsessed viewer) who discovers short-film creators online and wants to *support them with money*, not just a like.
- **Problem:** There is no single curated place to rent or buy access to indie shorts without subscribing to a big platform. Support today means either a subscription bundle where the creator sees pennies, or a tip jar with no film attached.
- **Position the brand wants:** A direct, curated, pay-per-film channel that treats the transaction as an act of patronage, not consumption.
- **Constraints:** Local-only, synthetic catalog, no real payment/provider, deterministic for the learning loop.

---

## Stage 1: Positioning territories

### Territory 1: The Patron's Channel *(Identity angle)* ★ recommended

- **Statement:** For people who want their money to reach the maker, not the platform.
- **Why this is true:** Pay-per-film, transparent split, the creator's name above the fold. No bundle math hiding who got paid.
- **What this rejects:** All-you-can-watch subscriptions; the "content library" frame; algorithmic feeds.
- **Risk:** "Patronage" can feel worthy/heavy. Must stay warm, not sanctimonious.

### Territory 2: The Curated Cut *(Originator angle)*

- **Statement:** The only place where every short is hand-picked, and the pick is the product.
- **Why this is true:** Small, editorially defended catalog; each film has a reason it's here.
- **What this rejects:** Infinite scroll, "more like this," volume-as-value.
- **Risk:** Curation is expensive and subjective; taste must be trusted or the whole thing collapses.

### Territory 3: Against the Bundle *(Antagonist angle)*

- **Statement:** The opposite of a streaming subscription — you buy the film, you own the moment, the maker gets paid now.
- **Why this is true:** One film, one price, one clear payout.
- **What this rejects:** Monthly recurring anything; "included with your plan."
- **Risk:** Defines the brand by the incumbent it hates; can read as reactive rather than its own thing.

### Territory 4: Discovery You Pay Forward *(Worldview angle)*

- **Statement:** We believe finding a filmmaker early should mean you can back them early.
- **Why this is true:** Ties the *discovery* moment directly to a *support* action in the same click.
- **What this rejects:** Passive discovery that ends in a bookmark; support decoupled from the work.
- **Risk:** Must be lived — needs real emerging creators, not established names, or the "early" claim is empty.

**Converge → Territory 1 (The Patron's Channel)**, borrowing the "buy the film, not the plan" sharpness from Territory 3 as the marketing edge.

### Round 3: top 3 with full case

#### Finalist 1: Onereel ★ recommended

- **Pronunciation:** "one-reel"
- **Type:** Compound / heritage term (one-reeler = a short film, ~10 min, one film reel)
- **Meaning:** The unit of a short film. Signals *shorts-first* and *one film at a time* — which is exactly the anti-bundle position.
- **Why it works:** Meaningful to film people, learnable to everyone; distinctive in the streaming category; stretches if the catalog grows to features ("beyond one reel"); warm, no negative reads.
- **Risks:** Users may render it "One Reel" — decide the canonical form early.
- **Visual potential:** Strong — the "O" as a film reel/aperture; monogram writes itself.

#### Finalist 2: Marquee

- **Pronunciation:** "mar-KEE"
- **Type:** Metaphor
- **Meaning:** The lit sign that puts a name up in front of the world — pure patronage energy.
- **Why it works:** Emotional, premium, ties support to visibility for the creator.
- **Risks:** Common English word → harder to own in search; some may misspell "marquis."
- **Visual potential:** High — theatre-marquee lighting, letterboard type.

#### Finalist 3: Spool

- **Pronunciation:** "spool"
- **Type:** Metaphor
- **Meaning:** The reel the film winds onto; compact, physical, a little nostalgic.
- **Why it works:** One syllable, easy wordmark, ownable in a tech field of coined vowel-salad names.
- **Risks:** Meaning isn't self-evident; needs a tagline to anchor.
- **Visual potential:** High — concentric spool/reel mark.

## Stage 3: Mood directions

### Mood 1: Archival Warmth ★ recommended

- **Description:** A film archive you'd actually want to sit in — cream, ink, one warm accent, generous space around each title.
- **Color cues:** Warm off-white/cream base, near-black ink, single warm accent (amber/ember). Muted, not saturated.
- **Typography cues:** A confident serif for titles (editorial, literary), clean humanist sans for UI.
- **Imagery direction:** Film stills treated large and respectful; grain welcome; each film gets room, not a thumbnail grid.
- **Reference brands:** Criterion Collection, MUBI (pared back), The New York Times Magazine, A24's editorial pages.
- **What this rejects:** Dark-mode "content wall," neon, dense poster grids, Netflix-style rows.

### Mood 2: Projection Booth

- **Description:** The romance of the mechanism — light, lens, dust in the beam. Darker, cinematic, focused.
- **Color cues:** Deep charcoal/black base, a single beam-of-light accent (warm white or gold).
- **Typography cues:** Geometric sans, tight; numerals like a film countdown.
- **Imagery direction:** High-contrast, spotlit stills; motion/light motifs.
- **Reference brands:** MUBI (full dark), Letterboxd (moodier corners), festival title cards.
- **What this rejects:** Cream/paper softness; playful color.

### Mood 3: Zine Handmade

- **Description:** Indie, made-by-humans, a little rough — like a film-club flyer stapled to a wall.
- **Color cues:** Paper white + one or two bold flat spot colors (risograph feel).
- **Typography cues:** Grotesk sans, oversized, some hand elements.
- **Imagery direction:** Cut-paper, halftone, DIY collage over stills.
- **Reference brands:** Risograph zines, indie festival branding, Bandcamp's maker-first warmth.
- **What this rejects:** Premium polish; serif gravitas.

**Converge → Mood 1 (Archival Warmth)**, closest to the patronage/curation position; Mood 3 (Zine) as the fallback if the brand should feel scrappier and more maker-adjacent.

## Stage 4: Narrative angles

### Angle 1: The Frustration Story ★ recommended

- **Premise:** You find a filmmaker you love online, you want to give them money for the actual film, and there is nowhere to do it. The category is broken; this is the fix.
- **First-page hero:** *"You found them first. Now back them — one film, straight to the maker."*
- **About-page opening:** "Every great director made shorts nobody could buy. We got tired of watching brilliant work vanish into a feed while the platform kept the money. So we built the smallest possible thing: a film, a price, a payout."
- **Why it works:** Matches the persona's lived frustration exactly; earns the anti-bundle position honestly.
- **Why it might fail:** Anger-forward stories date fast; must resolve into warmth, not just grievance.

### Angle 2: The Patron's Worldview

- **Premise:** Supporting art early used to be how art survived. We bring patronage to the click.
- **First-page hero:** *"Be the reason the next one gets made."*
- **About-page opening:** "Before studios, there were patrons — people who paid so the work could exist. We think that's still the best deal in art."
- **Why it works:** Dignified, ownable, aligns with Territory 1.
- **Why it might fail:** Can float above the product if the payout isn't visibly real.

### Angle 3: The Curator's Discovery

- **Premise:** We watch everything so you can back the few that matter.
- **First-page hero:** *"A short worth paying for. Every time."*
- **Why it works:** Sells the curation as the value.
- **Why it might fail:** Puts the brand's taste on trial with every pick.

---

## Synthesis: provisional recommendation

> **Provisional combination:** Name **Onereel**, territory **The Patron's Channel** (with the "buy the film, not the plan" edge), mood **Archival Warmth**, narrative **The Frustration Story**.
>
> **Why this combination:** The name carries the shorts-first, one-at-a-time meaning that IS the position, and the frustration story is the persona's exact pain — so the brand and the user's problem are the same sentence. Archival Warmth keeps it patronage, not consumption.
>
> **What we test next (in the WEAVE loop):** Use the hero copy and one-film-one-price framing as the baseline funnel; the first bounded adaptation candidate is the checkout/patronage-framing change measured against the frozen seeds.
