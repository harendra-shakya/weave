## Positioning: The Patron's Channel

**Statement:** For people who want their money to reach the maker, not the platform.

**Why it's true:** Pay-per-film. The creator's name above the fold. One film, one price — no bundle math hiding who got paid.

**What it rejects:**
- All-you-can-watch subscriptions
- The "content library" / infinite-feed frame
- Algorithmic rows and "more like this"
- Support decoupled from the actual work (bare tip jars)

**The sharp edge (marketing):** *Buy the film, not the plan.* Use as a secondary line, not the hero.

---

## Name: OneReel

- **Canonical form:** `OneReel` (never "One Reel" or "onereel" in brand surfaces)
- **Meaning:** A *one-reeler* is the classic short film — roughly ten minutes, one physical reel. The name IS the position: shorts-first, one at a time.
- **Pronunciation:** "one-reel"
- **Stretch:** Works if the catalog later grows ("beyond one reel") without renaming.
- **Wordmark potential:** The "O" as a film reel / projector aperture; monogram = **OR** or a single reel-O mark.

## Narrative: The Frustration Story

**Premise:** You find a filmmaker you love online. You want to give them money for the actual film. There's nowhere to do it — only subscriptions where the maker sees pennies, or a tip jar with no film attached. The category is broken. OneReel is the smallest possible fix: a film, a price, a payout.

**Hero (homepage):**
> # Be the reason the next one gets made.
> Back indie filmmakers you discover — one film at a time, straight to the maker.

**About-page opening:**
> Every great director made shorts nobody could buy. We got tired of watching brilliant work vanish into a feed while the platform kept the money. So we built the smallest possible thing: a film, a price, and a payout that goes straight to the maker.

**The hero (who the audience identifies with):** The viewer-as-patron — the person who found the filmmaker first and wants that discovery to *mean* something.

**Proof points (must stay true in the product):**
- The maker's name is always visible on the film.
- Support is one click from the film itself, never a detached donate button.
- "Straight to the maker" is stated qualitatively — **no invented percentage.**

## Mood: Archival Warmth

A film archive you'd actually want to sit in. Curated, warm, respectful of the work — the opposite of a dark content wall.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Base** | Warm off-white / cream |
| **Text** | Near-black ink |
| **Accent** | **Ember / burnt orange** — single accent, used sparingly (CTAs, the maker's name, active states) |
| **Saturation** | Muted, not neon |
| **Type — titles** | Confident editorial serif (literary/archival feel) |
| **Type — UI** | Clean humanist sans |
| **Imagery** | Film stills treated large and respectful; grain welcome; each film gets room, not a thumbnail grid |
| **Reference feel** | Criterion Collection, MUBI (pared back), NYT Magazine editorial, A24 editorial pages |

**What this rejects:** Dark-mode content wall, neon, dense poster grids, Netflix-style auto-scrolling rows.

---

## Voice quick-reference

- **Warm, not worthy.** Patronage without sanctimony. "Back them," not "Please consider supporting independent cinema."
- **Direct.** One film, one price, one maker. Short sentences.
- **The maker is the hero, the user is the patron.** Copy points at the filmmaker, not the platform.
- **Never:** "content," "titles library," "unlimited," "subscribe," "stream more."
